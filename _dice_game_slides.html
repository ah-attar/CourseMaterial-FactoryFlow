The Dice Game

Why a perfectly balanced line ships less than its capacity

Dependent events · statistical fluctuations · buffers

Introduce the game as a 20-minute experiment. Nothing is broken in this line: every station has the same capacity. The surprise is how much it still loses.
Before the game · where it comes from

Herbie and the hiking troop

In Goldratt's The Goal, plant manager Alex Rogo leads a scout hike. Every boy walks at about the same average pace, yet the line keeps stretching out and the troop arrives late.

Each boy can only walk as fast as the gap in front of him allows. A slow moment is passed back down the line; a fast moment is wasted if the boy in front is right there.

Before the game · the line

Four stations, one die each

Every station has the same capacity: a fair die averages 3.5 units per round. On paper the line is perfectly balanced, and a planner would expect 3.5 shipped per round.

Stress "balanced": a planner would say this line can ship 3.5 units per round.
Before the game · rules

How a round works

  1. Stations roll in order, from station 1 to the last.
  2. Your roll is the most units you may move from your input buffer to your output buffer.
  3. If your input buffer holds fewer units, move what is there. The rest of your roll is lost.
  4. Station 1 never runs short: it draws from unlimited raw material.
  5. Whatever the last station moves is shipped and counted.

No borrowing and no catching up: capacity you don't use this round is gone for good.

Read the rules once, then go straight to the worked example on the next slide.

After the game · why

Lost capacity cannot be made up

  • Dependent events. A station can only move what the one before it delivered.
  • Statistical fluctuations. A low roll starves the stations behind it; a high roll is capped by what is waiting.
  • Losses pass down the line and never come back, so every station's average falls below 3.5, and the last falls furthest.

Fluctuations do not average out in a chain of dependent steps. About 40 station-rounds in every 100 are starved.

Station 1 always averages 3.5 because it never runs out of raw material. Everything downstream inherits the lost capacity.

After the game · what TOC says to do

The five focusing steps

  1. Identify the constraint: the step that limits the whole line.
  2. Exploit it: never let it starve or work on the wrong thing.
  3. Subordinate everything else: release work at the constraint's pace (drum-buffer-rope).
  4. Elevate it: add capacity only once it is fully used.
  5. Repeat: when the constraint moves, start again.

A balanced line has no fixed constraint, so it moves from round to round. Better: one deliberate constraint, a buffer in front of it, and releases tied to its pace.

Link back to the push vs pull session: drum-buffer-rope is a pull release paced by the constraint.
Takeaways

Three things to remember

  • 1Balanced capacity is not balanced flow. With dependency and variation, the line ships less than any one station's average.
  • 2Capacity lost at one station is lost for the whole line. It cannot be recovered later.
  • 3Buffers protect throughput but cost money. Put them in front of the constraint, not everywhere.
